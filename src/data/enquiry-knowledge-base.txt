TITLE: Changing Owners Corporation Manager
CATEGORY: New Client
CONTENT:
Strata Management Consultants helps Owners Corporation Committees change OC managers by guiding them through a structured process.
The process involves five steps:
1. Preliminary no-obligation meeting with the OC Committee to discuss the building and management.
2. Review of key documents to help the Committee understand timing, key dates, and costs of the incumbent OC management company.
3. Site visit to better understand the building in conjunction with the Plan of Subdivision; scoping session with the Committee to scope and detail what they are looking for.
4. Assistance to the Committee to conduct the tender process for a new OC management company.
5. Follow-up meeting with the Committee to review new management proposals and discuss next steps and action points.
The company has researched and vetted OC management companies in Melbourne to help Committees avoid unsuitable or inexperienced managers. The goal is to help Committees change once and do it right.

TITLE: What Is a Body Corporate
CATEGORY: General Question
CONTENT:
A body corporate is a legal entity created when land is subdivided and common property exists.
In Victoria, the term "body corporate" was replaced by "Owners Corporation" when the Owners Corporations Act 2006 came into effect on 1 January 2007.
An Owners Corporation manages common property and shared obligations within the property.

TITLE: Is Body Corporate Compulsory in Victoria
CATEGORY: General Question
CONTENT:
In Victoria, an Owners Corporation is automatically created when a property is subdivided into lots with shared or common areas.
The required management structure and procedures depend on the size and type of Owners Corporation.
Staff should provide general guidance only and recommend professional advice for legal or compliance decisions.

TITLE: Becoming a Registered OC Manager in Victoria
CATEGORY: General Question
CONTENT:
To become a registered Owners Corporation manager in Victoria, a person or company generally needs to:
- Hold professional indemnity insurance
- Meet eligibility requirements
- Apply through Consumer Affairs Victoria
- Pay the required registration fee
The registration application fee was $239.70 for the 2025–26 financial year.
Staff should clarify that registration requirements can change and clients should check Consumer Affairs Victoria for current official requirements.

TITLE: Water Damage Responsibility
CATEGORY: Maintenance Issue
CONTENT:
Responsibility for water damage in a Victorian strata property depends on where the damage originates and whether it involves common property or a private lot.
The Owners Corporation is generally responsible for maintaining and repairing common property and related shared services.
Common property may include:
- Shared plumbing or electrical systems
- Pathways, foyers, and entryways
- Building exterior, roof, and balconies
- Lifts, stairwells, driveways, and hallways
- Gardens, swimming pools, and shared amenities
Lot owners may be responsible when damage originates inside their private lot, such as:
- Leaks from fixtures inside the lot
- Mildew caused by internal activities
- Faulty renovations by the owner
- Services that exclusively serve the lot
Recommended staff action:
Ask the client to document the issue, take photos, identify where the water appears to be coming from, and report it to the OC manager or Committee.

TITLE: Water Damage Next Steps
CATEGORY: Maintenance Issue
CONTENT:
If a client reports water damage, staff should recommend these steps:
1. Take photos and notes showing the damage.
2. Report the issue to the strata Committee or OC manager.
3. Identify whether the issue involves common property or a private lot.
4. Review the plan of subdivision, insurance coverage, and legal responsibilities.
5. For complex cases, ask for an expert inspection.
If the client says their current manager is not responding or not handling the issue properly, classify the enquiry as a Maintenance Issue or Complaint depending on tone.

TITLE: Complaints About Current OC Manager
CATEGORY: Complaint
CONTENT:
If a client complains about poor service from their current OC manager, staff should classify the enquiry as Complaint.
Common complaint signals:
- No replies or poor communication
- Delays and unresolved maintenance issues
- Overcharging concerns or lack of transparency
- Inexperienced or underqualified manager
- Dishonest, toxic, or intimidating management behaviour
- Financial mismanagement causing significant losses
- Committee dissatisfaction
Recommended staff action:
Ask for the building size, current manager, main issues, and whether the Committee is considering changing or reviewing the manager.

TITLE: Urgent or Emergency Enquiry
CATEGORY: Urgent / Emergency
CONTENT:
Classify as Urgent / Emergency when the enquiry mentions:
- OC manager resigning unexpectedly or giving very short notice
- Building needing urgent replacement management
- Immediate safety risks such as flooding, fire, electrical danger, burst pipes, or serious water leaks
- Lockouts affecting safety
- Immediate building safety concerns
Recommended staff action:
Advise the client to contact emergency services or the appropriate urgent building contact if there is immediate danger. Route the enquiry for urgent human review. These enquiries should be reviewed promptly by staff.
